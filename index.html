Why Use a Land Trust?

Land trusts provide powerful benefits for real estate investors and homeowners alike

🛡

Due-on-Sale Protection

Transferring property into a land trust is specifically exempted from triggering the due-on-sale clause under the Garn-St. Germain Act of 1982.

🕵

Privacy & Anonymity

The trust name appears on public records instead of your personal name, keeping your ownership private from public databases and title searches.

⚖

Lawsuit Protection

Assets held in a land trust are more difficult to discover and attach in lawsuits, providing an additional layer of protection against frivolous claims.

🔄

Easy Transfers

Change beneficial interest without recording a new deed. Transfer ownership quickly and privately by simply assigning the beneficiary interest.

💰

Avoid Probate

Property held in a land trust passes directly to successor beneficiaries without going through the costly and time-consuming probate process.

🏢

Multiple Properties

Each property can be held in its own separate trust, isolating liability. A problem with one property doesn't affect your other holdings.

Insurance Instructions

Critical insurance requirements for subject-to purchases

01 Select Your Policy Type

🏠
Landlord Policy
▼

Required if you plan on renting the property.

⚡
Builder's All Risk
▼

Required if the property needs to be renovated first.

🏢
Vacant Home Policy
▼

Required if the property will be vacant or up for sale.

🛡
Homeowner's Policy
▼

If you will live there as Beneficiary. Have "Beneficiary Domiciles" on the deed.

02 Critical Insurance Rules

Do NOT use the existing Homeowner's policy — you are not the insured as the deed has changed.

Do NOT have 2 policies existing simultaneously — your claim can be denied.

Have your policy started the day of closing.

Do NOT cancel existing policy until you close — closings get delayed.

The Insured is the Trustee: Fiduciary Asset Protection Group, LLC (not the Trust — the Trust is a document).

Name the Bank as the 1st mortgagee.

Sellers as Additional Interest (NOT Additionally Insured) — seller's name will not be on insurance claim check.

Cover Beneficiary with General Liability Insurance — declaration page is NOT sent to the bank.

Cover the Seller with General Liability Insurance — and send that to the bank.

If existing policy is escrowed (PiTi), have the insurance company send the invoice to the bank to be paid.

You must have a Durable Power of Attorney (POA) to sign claim checks with the seller's name.

⚠️

YOU SHOULD NEVER CLOSE WITHOUT INSURANCE

The existing seller's insurance does NOT cover you.
